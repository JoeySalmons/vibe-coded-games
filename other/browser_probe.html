    What this page can learn about you without asking.

    Open a web page and it can quietly read dozens of facts about your device — your screen, your GPU, your fonts, your timezone, your network — and combine them into a fingerprint that follows you across sites, no cookies required. This page runs those same checks on you, right now, and shows the raw values, what they mean, and how identifying each one is. Nothing is sent anywhere except the optional public-IP lookup, which is clearly marked.

    This signature is built only from the probes below, hashed together — it is not stored or transmitted. The “bits” figure is an illustrative estimate of how rare your configuration is, not a measured population statistic. Real fingerprinting services compare against millions of visitors.

    How to read this: Each probe shows a status — Worked, Partial, Blocked / failed, or Unsupported — plus raw data, a plain-English reading, and a one-line note on what it reveals.

    Why some checks differ when you open this as a local file (file://) versus host it (e.g. GitHub Pages over HTTPS):

    Built as a teaching tool. To reduce your real-world fingerprint, the strongest moves are using a browser that resists fingerprinting (Tor Browser normalises most of these values; Brave and Firefox add noise to canvas/audio), keeping your browser updated, and being cautious with permissions. Inspired by the techniques catalogued at EFF’s Cover Your Tracks and BrowserLeaks.
